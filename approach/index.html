Our approach

Start with questions.
Follow the evidence.

We want to understand people’s constraints before selecting solutions—and understand what changes before claiming impact.

A research philosophy

Listening is
part of the method.

Research should be useful to the people whose lives it describes.

Our intended process combines community knowledge with consistent measurement. Local partners help shape the questions, interpret findings, and identify what a proposed intervention would mean in practice.

Collecting more data is not the same as making a better decision. We need a clear purpose for each measure, a feasible way to learn from it, and a responsible plan for using the findings.

The learning cycle

Six steps. Room to reconsider.

01

Listen

Learn from people experiencing poverty and organizations already working alongside them. Establish priorities together.

02

Measure

Define relevant welfare measures, establish a baseline, and collect only the information needed for the research purpose.

03

Understand

Examine patterns and alternative explanations. Interpret constraints in the context of households and communities.

04

Test

Develop a feasible intervention with local partners. Agree on responsibilities, consent, safeguards, and an evaluation design before starting.

05

Evaluate

Track welfare over time. Use a credible comparison or another justified research design when estimating attributable impact.

06

Improve

Share what was learned, including limitations and disappointing results. Adapt, stop, or scale according to the evidence.

Three different questions

Be precise about progress.

01 / REACH

Who participated?

The people or households enrolled or contacted. Reach describes activity; it does not establish a welfare improvement.

02 / OBSERVED CHANGE

What improved?

Measured changes in welfare over time. Changes may reflect an intervention, outside events, or both.

03 / ATTRIBUTABLE IMPACT

What did the intervention cause?

The change credibly linked to an intervention using an appropriate evaluation design, with uncertainty reported.

Before fieldwork, welfare measures, poverty thresholds, reference periods, and follow-up plans must be defined for the local context. We do not yet report verified participant outcomes.
